Shipments stopped Organisations in Ukraine's energy and finance sectors were crippled, and the malware spread to businesses across Europe.

  1. 1

    IT and cloud breached

    Destructive malware was launched in Ukraine on 27 June 2017.

    UK: the Russian military

  2. 2

    Electricity target

    The primary targets were Ukraine's financial, energy and government sectors.

  3. 3

    Food shipments stopped

    The malware spread beyond Ukraine. At Mondelez, sales, distribution and finance networks stopped, and shipments and invoicing stood still for the last four days of the quarter.

What limited the damage: Continuity plans; Mondelez caught up on the delayed shipments in the following quarter.

Remote control lost Remote control of about 5,800 wind turbines was lost for weeks, although the energy company was not the target.

  1. 1

    Telecoms breached

    Into the management network through the satellite operator's misconfigured VPN appliance; from there the memory of tens of thousands of modems was overwritten on 24 Feb 2022.

    Viasat KA-SAT; AcidRain

  2. 2

    Electricity link cut

    Remote monitoring and control of about 5,800 wind turbines was lost.

What limited the damage: The turbines kept running automatically. Some parks moved to the mobile network.

Stores closed About 700 of Coop's 800 stores in Sweden closed; all were open again within six days.

  1. 1

    IT and cloud breached

    The attacker used zero-day vulnerabilities in remote management software on 2 July 2021.

    Kaseya VSA; REvil

  2. 2

    IT and cloud spread

    A service company that maintains stores' tills through remote management spread the malware to its customers.

    Visma EssCom

  3. 3

    Food closed

    Tills and self-checkouts were encrypted, and the stores could not take payments.

What limited the damage: Over 700 store servers were reinstalled through automation; customer data was not affected.

Visma: Kaseya-attacken (3.7.2021)

Done by hand A single data centre took down healthcare and central bank systems on the same night.

  1. 1

    IT and cloud encrypted

    Ransomware hit an IT service provider's data centre in Sweden on the night of 20 January 2024.

    Tietoevry; Akira

  2. 2

    Healthcare by hand

    In the Uppsala region, patient records, the finance system and supply ordering were handled by hand.

  3. 3

    Banking and payments disrupted

    The Riksbank's payroll system was unavailable; critical financial system services were not affected.

What limited the damage: Fallback procedures; over 90% of servers were restored from backups in four days.

Access to medicines hampered In early May, payment traffic was still at 86% of normal.

  1. 1

    Banking and payments breached

    Stolen credentials to the remote access portal of a healthcare payments clearinghouse that had no multi-factor authentication (12 Feb 2024).

    Citrix; ALPHV/BlackCat

  2. 2

    Healthcare stopped

    Ransomware on 21 Feb 2024 stopped billing, payments and pharmacy claims.

What limited the damage: The pharmacy network was restored first, 99% of pharmacies by 7 March, and traffic was routed to competitors.

Andrew Witty, written testimony to the US Senate (2024)

Not an attack A faulty update, not an attack, but the same node and the same consequence across sectors.

  1. 1

    IT and cloud faulty update

    A faulty security software update crashed about 8.5 million Windows devices in July 2024.

  2. 2

    Banking and payments outages

    Account access, payments, cash withdrawals and online banking logins were disrupted.

  3. 3

    Healthcare on paper

    In England, the appointment and patient record system was disrupted at most GP practices, which used paper records and handwritten prescriptions.

    EMIS

What limited the damage: Most financial institutions recovered the same day; most hospital care continued as normal.
