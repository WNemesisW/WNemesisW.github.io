Model

  • State of the environment – Situation awareness always concerns an environment that also changes as a result of one's own actions. (Endsley 1995)
  • Perception – Perceiving the relevant elements of the situation and their status and attributes. (Endsley 1995)
  • Comprehension – Combining perceptions into meaning in the light of one's goals. (Endsley 1995)
  • Projection – Projecting the near future, which makes it possible to act before the situation unfolds. (Endsley 1995)
  • Attention and working memory – Working memory holds only a few items at a time, and tasks using the same resource compete with each other. (Cowan 2001, Wickens 2008)
  • Mental models and goals – Models built from experience tell how to combine perceptions and what follows from them. A wrong model biases comprehension and projection. (Endsley 1995, Endsley & Jones 2025, Jones: SA demons)
  • Tools and automation – The interface and automation determine what can be perceived at all. Automation can take the human out of the loop. (Endsley 1996, Endsley 2017)
  • Shared situation awareness – Situation awareness is distributed across people and artefacts. Each needs their own part, not everything. (Stanton et al. 2006, Salmon et al. 2008)
  • Action – Action changes the environment, and feedback starts the cycle again. (Endsley 1995)
  • Decision – Situation awareness is a prerequisite for a decision, not the decision itself. An experienced decision-maker recognises the situation and picks the first workable option. (Endsley 1995, Endsley 2015, Klein & Klinger 1991)

Four directions of shared situation awareness (Drury et al. 2006)

  • Human about device – Location, health, task and its progress, projection and weather.
  • Human about human – The location, role and activities of other operators and leaders.
  • Device about human – Commands given and constraints set by humans.
  • Device about device – Other devices' plans, contingencies and re-tasking.
