Some terminals and bookings down Maersk estimated the cost at 250–300 million dollars.

  1. 1

    Supplier breached

    A backdoor had been planted in an update of a Ukrainian accounting program, and the update spread the malware.

    NotPetya, M.E.Doc (US-CERT TA17-181A)

  2. 2

    Office wiped

    The malware spread through the network and wiped the machines. The network was rebuilt: about 4,000 servers and 45,000 PCs.

    news source

  3. 3

    Systems down

    The booking system went down, and new bookings were taken through other channels.

    news source

  4. 4

    Port partly stopped

    Some terminals were disrupted, and work was done by hand. According to the company, most terminals operated on contingency plans.

    17 of 76 terminals according to Wired news source

Why this belongs in the chain: One supplier's update spread the malware across the whole company network. Recovery was made possible by a domain controller that was offline because of a power cut.

Fuel supply cut The pipeline was shut from 7 to 12 May 2021 and returned to normal on 15 May.

  1. 1

    Perimeter breached

    The attacker logged in with an old VPN account that had no multi-factor authentication.

    Senate hearing 8 June 2021

  2. 2

    Office encrypted

    Ransomware encrypted IT systems. No signs of intrusion were found in the control systems.

    DarkSide (CISA AA21-131A)

  3. 3

    Terminal stopped

    The control room shut the pipeline down as a precaution, and some control systems were disconnected.

  4. 4

    Distribution stations dry

    Many stations ran out of fuel, and the average petrol price rose above three dollars a gallon.

    EIA 18 May 2021

Why this belongs in the chain: The intrusion was limited to IT, but supply stopped because of the precautionary shutdown.

Terminals at limited capacity Loading was disrupted for more than a week. Other suppliers and depots filled the gap.

  1. 1

    Office breached

    On 29 January 2022, Oiltanking and Mabanaft detected a cyber incident affecting their IT systems.

  2. 2

    Terminal limited

    The German tank terminals operated at limited capacity, and the company declared force majeure. The attack hit loading and unloading systems.

  3. 3

    Distribution disrupted

    Mabanaft declared force majeure on inland deliveries. According to the BSI, 233 stations were disrupted.

    news source

What limited the damage: Other suppliers and depots filled the gap. According to dpa, the BSI assessed the situation as serious but not grave.

Trains stopped In October 2022, many trains stood still for about four hours.

  1. 1

    Supplier breached

    Hackers broke into a test server of the supplier of the train crews' app.

    Supeo (news sources)

  2. 2

    Transport down

    The supplier shut down its production servers as a precaution. The offline version of the drivers' app did not work.

  3. 3

    Transport stopped

    The trains were stopped in line with safety procedures.

Why this belongs in the chain: A break-in at the supplier's test environment stopped production because the fallback did not work. DSB fixed offline use through testing.

Four ports stopped The ports reopened on 13 November 2023, and a backlog of about 30,000 containers was cleared by 20 November.

  1. 1

    Systems breached

    On 10 November 2023, DP World Australia detected unauthorised access to its network. The way in has not been disclosed.

  2. 2

    Port stopped

    The company disconnected its network from the internet. Trucks could not deliver or collect containers at four ports.

  3. 3

    Office data taken

    The attacker accessed some files and took a small amount of data, including employees' personal data. No ransomware was used.

Why this belongs in the chain: No ransomware was used, but the company's own containment decision – cutting the network off from the internet – stopped the ports' landside operations.

Five terminals stopped Container traffic was stopped from 4 to 6 July 2023, for about three days.

  1. 1

    Perimeter breached

    The most likely route was a VPN device used for maintenance access that had no IP restriction, had unpatched known vulnerabilities and whose logs were not collected.

    Japan's transport ministry (MLIT) review

  2. 2

    Systems encrypted

    All servers of the terminal system were encrypted, and a ransom note was printed on the system's printer.

  3. 3

    Port stopped

    Container moves in and out of five terminals stopped. Recovery was delayed because malware was found in the backup.

    news source

Why this belongs in the chain: The external connection used for maintenance had no IP restriction, and the VPN device's logs were not collected.

How to prevent similar incidents

Public guidance and good practice that matches the steps of the case.

  • 1

    Patch the maintenance VPN appliance and keep its logs

    Kyberturvallisuuskeskus: Kyberturvallisuuden vahvistaminen suomalaisissa organisaatiossa, kohta 3.7 (2022) ↗

  • 1

    Collect logs from all edge devices centrally

    Kyberturvallisuuskeskus: Näin keräät ja käytät lokitietoja (2020) ↗

  • 3

    Test backups and check them for malware

    CISA & MS-ISAC: #StopRansomware Guide (2020, updated 2023) ↗
